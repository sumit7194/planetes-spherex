Planetes

About Planetes

Planetes is a public web tool for NASA's SPHEREx mission. Type a position in the sky and it plays SPHEREx's infrared photos of that spot, pass by pass, so anyone can see what moved or changed: nearby stars, brown dwarfs, stars that brighten and fade. It was built by team Planetes (Sumit, Gorakhpur, India) for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, challenge “Planet X and SPHEREx”.

How to read what you see

  • Ember: measured by this tool (rough centroids and quick aperture photometry, with their scatter).
  • Dashed lilac: predicted from a catalogue's proper motion (Gaia DR3, or the literature for WISE 0855).
  • Red outlines: pixels the SPHEREx pipeline flagged as saturated. They are outlined, never hidden; their values are not measurements.
  • Grey: masked pixels (flagged artefacts) in difference images.
  • SPHEREx pixels are 6.15″ across. Barnard's Star moves about 10″ a year, so under two pixels: zoom in and watch the ring.

Inspiration

WiseView (byw.tools/wiseview) and the Backyard Worlds: Planet 9 project, where volunteers found many brown dwarfs by blinking WISE images.

Prior work and AI use

The idea is Sumit's. He prepared before the event in private practice projects; what this project reuses from them (a stretch module, design tokens, a design language and tested archive recipes) is listed in PRIOR_WORK.md. Everything else was written during the hackathon.

This project was built with substantial help from Claude (Anthropic), an AI assistant, which wrote most of the code and draft text under the team's direction and review. Data handling and the numbers shown were checked against IRSA's documentation and catalogue predictions. No AI-generated images, audio or video are used.
